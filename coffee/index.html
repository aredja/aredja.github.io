Coffee Map

Coffee places worth to think about visiting.

Thinking in a café is quite hard, if the only thing you are thinking about is how terrible your coffee tastes. This personal coffee map is inspired by Ariel Rubinstein’s Coffee Atlas. I am no coffee expert, but I do enjoy rambling about what a very good cappuccino or espresso should look and taste like. Unless stated otherwise, I always order a cappucino.

All ratings are, of course, entirely subjective. They may depend on the barista, the exact time of the visit, and my personal mood at the time. Alongside coffee, atmosphere, and service, I also rate each café’s suitability for thinking up your next research idea, only to discard it the following morning. The final grade is not a mechanical average of the reported categories: it contains omitted variables and is ultimately based on pure gut feeling.

Le Petit Marais

Zurich, Switzerland

Cappuccino: CHF 5.80

Last visit: June 2026

8.1 / 10
Coffee
7.7
Atmosphere
8.2
Service
8.4

Le Petit Marais set out to bring the French art of viennoiserie to Zurich. Its name pays homage to Le Marais, the fashionable and historic Parisian district. This is rather amusing given that the café is located in Altstetten, Zurich's ninth district, which is famous for being almost everything except fashionable and historic, unless working-class heritage and concrete apartment blocks count. But this is neither a lecture on Altstetten nor an inquiry into the proper use of place names. It is about coffee, and Le Petit Marais continues to leave my palate confused. After four years of loyal visits, one might expect the quality of its cappuccino to have converged towards a steady state. Unfortunately, it still oscillates between "so good that it even overshadows the phenomenal vermicelles" and "I could have bought something useless at the Brocki next door with this money," conditional on which barista prepares it. The price, however, appears to follow a much clearer trend and shows no signs of being bounded above. I admit that I am biased: considered as a whole, the café is a must-visit for its pastries and sandwiches. But because this coffee map primarily judges the cappuccino, the lack of consistency weighs heavily on the coffee score. Even so, after four years as a loyal customer, it is clearly not a good enough reason to stop visiting.

The pastries are so good that they make repeated cappuccino disappointments a rational choice.

BASƎ

Verona, Italy

Cappuccino: €3.50

Last visit: May 2025

9.3 / 10
Coffee
9.5
Atmosphere
8.2
Service
9.4

Verona has no shortage of beauty: the UNESCO-listed historic centre, Piazza delle Erbe, the Roman Ponte Pietra, rebuilt after its destruction during the Second World War, and, of course, the Arena di Verona. Yet a fascinatingly large share of the city's visitors appear to prefer spending their time queuing to stare at a balcony. Speaking of bella Italia and its celebrated lifestyle, anyone expecting another enthusiastic account of the classical Italian café will be disappointed. BASƎ, formerly and at the time of my visit known as Garage Coffee Bros., may be known for many things, but certainly not for over-roasted beans, pre-ground coffee, or sour espresso. Many people will disagree with my assessment of the average Italian coffee experience, although I strongly suspect that their judgement is affected by holiday bias. BASƎ is nevertheless an essential stop in Verona. Coffee preparation is treated with remarkable seriousness and genuine care. The shop near the Arena is small and offers only a few seats, but it is thoughtfully and warmly designed. Rather than simply receiving whichever espresso is used for your cappuccino, you can choose the beans for the shot yourself. The staff explain their cultivation, roasting, and preparation, and you can smell the different coffees before deciding. You may have noticed by now that I thoroughly enjoyed the experience, which was completed by an excellent cappuccino. The coffee is comparatively expensive, but for anyone seeking something closer to a specialty coffee experience in Verona, there exists at least one café that checks all the boxes. Perhaps that explains the new name.

If you are more into filter coffee, the shop also offers a variety of brewing methods and beans to choose from.
